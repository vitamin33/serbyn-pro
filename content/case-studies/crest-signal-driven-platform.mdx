## The Challenge

Crest is built as a content marketing platform for indie hackers. Today it runs my own LinkedIn and X marketing. The job: find topics worth writing about, draft posts, publish them, and learn which posts work.

Two things make this hard. Model costs add up fast when every step calls an LLM. And a content engine that invents numbers is worse than no engine at all, because it publishes claims nobody can back up.

## Architecture

### Platform

Crest is one FastAPI service plus Celery workers on Redis, with APScheduler for the scheduled jobs. Data lives in PostgreSQL, and Qdrant handles duplicate detection. A Next.js 15 app is the admin. Everything runs as Docker Compose on AWS Lightsail, at api.usecrest.io and usecrest.io.

The first version, started in July 2025, was a set of separate services with a LangGraph drafting pipeline. As the product settled, the services were folded into one API and the workers.

### From research to a published post

A scheduled research engine pulls posts from Reddit and Hacker News, removes duplicates, then scores and ranks the topics. This step uses no LLM generation.

From the ranked list I pick what gets written, through a tool call or the approval queue in Telegram. Before any draft is written, a check looks at the brief. The draft then goes through editorial checks, including the fabrication checks below. Approved posts go to LinkedIn and X. Telegram only carries alerts and approvals.

### Multi-Model Routing

Each call goes to a model picked for the job. The first version had two models: GPT-4o for hooks and GPT-3.5-turbo for the body. The router today has three tiers and six providers:

- **Published posts and replies**: Claude, Sonnet 4.5 by default and Sonnet 5 for comments. This tier never falls back to a free model. If Claude and OpenAI both fail, the call errors and nothing weaker gets posted.
- **Independent review**: GPT-4o checks the text Claude wrote. A reviewer from a different model family does not share the writer's blind spots.
- **Extraction and internal jobs**: gpt-oss-120b on the free Groq and Cerebras tiers first, then DeepSeek and GPT-4o-mini as cheap paid fallbacks. Gemini 2.5 Flash handles structured JSON extraction.

A provider that runs out of quota goes on a cooldown, and the next one in the chain takes the call. Every call records its model and cost.

### Fabrication checks

Drafts are scored by an evaluator that runs blind, with two independent judges. In a calibration run it failed a draft with invented numbers ("847" and "12%") and passed a fully grounded one. That check matters more than any style score: a wrong number in a published post costs more than a dull sentence.

### Thompson Sampling for variant choice

Which variant, post type and posting hour to use is picked by Thompson Sampling. Each option keeps a Beta distribution of its past results, and the system samples from them, so options that did well get picked more often. By default 30% of picks go to exploration. While there are fewer than 20 observations, it uses UCB1 instead, which handles the cold start. Results from published posts update the distributions.

The bandit code has 165 tests across 18 test files. It can only learn as fast as engagement data comes back from the platforms.

### Cost and observability

Every LLM call lands in a cost ledger with its model, tokens and cost. Langfuse traces the calls and has dashboards for the cost trend, cost per feature and the quality trend, with alerts on daily cost and quality drops. The API also exposes Prometheus metrics.

## Results

As of August 2026, Crest has generated and scored 1,455 marketing posts, and 109 of them are published. Behind them are 9,001 judge scores and 205 research runs. It runs live at usecrest.io on AWS Lightsail.

Premium content never falls back to a free model. A provider outage stops the post instead.

## Key Learnings

**Route by job.** Published text goes to the strongest writer, and review goes to a model from a different family. Extraction and other internal work runs on free open-weight models. When the premium providers are down, the call fails loudly instead of quietly posting weaker text.

**Check the engine for fabrication before trusting it.** The calibration run with an invented "847" and "12%" is the test I trust most. A judge that passes made-up numbers is worse than no judge.

**Simplify when the product settles.** The first version had separate services and a LangGraph pipeline. One API plus workers is less to deploy and less to watch.
